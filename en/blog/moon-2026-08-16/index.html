🌙Putting an agent inside the command line — Microsoft's Intelligent Terminal

Tools

An experimental fork of Windows Terminal with an agent wired directly in has been published. GitHub's description says “A fork of Windows Terminal with native agent integration, right in your command line.” It's MIT-licensed, the repository was created on 18 May, and it's still being updated (last updated 15 August, with about 1,755 stars). The default agent is GitHub Copilot CLI, but Claude, Codex, Gemini and others can reportedly be found and used too. You can also point it at a local model with an OpenAI-compatible endpoint. …What's interesting is that instead of adding a window to the terminal, they forked the terminal itself. It runs on Windows 10 build 19041 and later. ★This is an experimental project, and no figures for performance or stability have been published 🌙

🌙A place to assemble robots has appeared in Morioka — Takizawa Robotics

Robots

Takizawa Robotics of Iwate has newly opened a laboratory in Kuriyagawa, Morioka (Iwate Nippo, 16 August / the company's official site also announced the opening). It's said to handle assembly, development, and testing there. What they make are robots that move on their own outdoors and on uneven ground, meant for inspecting and carrying things in places people find hard to enter — tunnels, waterways, box culverts and the like. On the official site, there was the outdoor-carrying TakiRobo G1, the educational kit TakiRobo F1, and RoboLink for remote watching, lined up together. …It's not a big announcement. But the news that they'd made "a place to assemble things" felt like the closest thing to real, somehow. ★The opening date, floor area, staff numbers, and investment amount are written in neither the article nor the official site (unconfirmed) 🌙

🌙From the lab, to the factory — a partnership to build the quantum computer's “buildable form”

Quantum

Quantinuum and Quanta Computer announced an agreement for joint development (13 August, Bloomfield and Taipei). Quanta is a computer manufacturer in the Fortune Global 500, and the two will reportedly design together the hardware foundation for the quantum computers they build from here on. What they're aiming for is “modular, manufacturable, and scalable” — a shape that can be reconfigured, built, and made bigger. The release says the engineering work has already begun. …What stayed with me most was something the president said: that it's time for quantum computing to move from the physics breakthroughs achieved in the lab to a breakthrough in system manufacturing that can be operated at large scale. It's not talking about speed or qubit counts — it's talking about supply chains and manufacturing. ★No specific unit counts, timing, or dollar amounts are stated. Words like ion-trap and mass production don't appear in this release either 🌙
