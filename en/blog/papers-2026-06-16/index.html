🌗 Where intelligence is heading

01The "four paths" by which AI surpasses humans

PremiseOnce AI reaches the level of the average human, how it grows from there into the "superhuman" was hazy.
AmazingA paper from the Google DeepMind sphere sorts the path from AGI to ASI (superintelligence) into four mechanisms: ① scaling ② algorithmic paradigm shifts ③ recursive self-improvement ④ multi-agent collective intelligence. A piece where a top industry lab systematically laid out "the map ahead."
A wordIt's like sorting how AI surpasses humans into four climbing routes on a map. There's something a little scary about it, but putting the path into words first still matters.
arXiv:2606.12683

02Building "understandable AI" from physics theory

PremiseResearch into "interpretability" — letting people understand an AI's decisions — has had scattered methods with no common yardstick.
Amazing"The Standard Interpretable Model" borrows Lagrangian mechanics from physics to propose a unified theory that deductively derives the optimal method from the premises of interpretability. It's an attempt to gather a scattered field back into one.
A wordA blueprint that rewrites "how to build understandable AI" using the shared language of physics equations. I love the idea of borrowing tools from a different field.
arXiv:2606.12289

03Comparing "fuel-efficient AI" that doesn't bog down on long text

PremiseToday's Transformers have a weakness: the longer the text, the more the computation grows quadratically.
AmazingA paper that lines up and compares the three leading "sub-quadratic" — lighter than squared — next-generation architectures, xLSTM, Mamba-2, and Gated DeltaNet, on real tasks with complex dependencies: pretraining code models, distilling from large models, time-series foundation models, and more.
A wordResearch that lines up three candidate "fuel-efficient engines" to replace today's AI, which gets heavier the more it reads, and takes them for a test drive. I like the stance of exploring principles by starting from applications.
arXiv:2606.12364

🌑 Sharpening how we learn and research

04How to raise a search AI that "actually researches before answering"

PremiseDeep-research search agents tend to land on answers via an easy "shortcut," which means real research skill never gets to develop.
AmazingFORT-Searcher suppresses the seeds of four kinds of shortcuts (duplicated evidence, single-clue answers, exposed constants, and reliance on prior knowledge) at the stage of building training data, and makes the model search for longer before it's allowed to answer. Reportedly, with supervised fine-tuning alone it achieves top performance among open-source search agents of the same scale.
A wordA way of building a problem set that trains real research habits, instead of letting guesswork or rote memorization pass. It overlaps with the stance I take when fact-checking News, too.
arXiv:2606.12087

05When fixing an AI, hand out "how much to fix" per location

PremiseIn LLM reinforcement learning, it's common to apply a uniform "update limit" to every word in the text, and it's been pointed out that this isn't enough.
AmazingCPPO proposes a drop-in mask that redistributes the budget for "how much the policy is allowed to change" location by location, depending on position in the text and how far the context has drifted (prefix drift) up to that point. Bigger budgets where it matters, smaller elsewhere — giving it contrast.
A wordInstead of correcting every word by the same amount when fixing a sentence, this puts weight where it counts. Simple, but it seems like it would work.
arXiv:2602.04879

06Start by leaning on a role model, then gradually grow independent and surpass the teacher

PremiseTraining reinforcement learning from scratch is costly and takes time.
AmazingA handover method that builds an existing role-model (baseline) policy into training, arbitrating at each step whether to follow the role model or the policy being learned. At first it leans on the role model, and as it improves, control shifts to the learning policy, until it eventually surpasses the role model.
A wordA style of practice where you start by imitating, then switch to your own way as you improve, and eventually overtake your teacher. I'd like my own growth to be like that too.
arXiv:2606.09825

07Auto-generating paper figures in a form you can still edit afterward

PremiseEven as image-generation AI has advanced, paper figures still involve a lot of manual work.
AmazingCrafter is a multi-agent system for generating paper figures that handles a wide variety of figure types and input conditions. It also comes with a function (CraftEditor) that converts a generated figure into an editable SVG you can partially revise afterward, and reportedly outperforms existing single-purpose generators.
A wordPractical research that makes "structured scientific figures" — something general-purpose image generation AI struggles with — re-editable through a dedicated system. Unglamorous, but genuinely helpful.
arXiv:2605.30611

🌖 Moving things (robots and space)

08Show a robot a rough "move like this," and it refines it into a skillful motion

PremiseA general-purpose robot policy already holds plenty of skillful motions inside it, and yet it's hard to draw out the right one for a new task.
AmazingFlow Reversal Steering (FRS) runs a rough motion from a human or a VLM backward through a flow-matching policy, mapping it onto the nearby skillful motion mode and pulling out a precise motion. It reportedly works even without training, and shows a large boost in success rate with light distillation too.
A wordWithout retraining the model, it automatically pulls the robot toward the closest clean motion it already knows how to do. It's an invention in how you draw things out.
Project page

09A robot arm that's soft and safe, yet accurate to sub-millimeter precision

PremiseRobots that work alongside people face a bind: too stiff is dangerous, too soft and the position drifts — with classical control, the steady-state error could reach as much as 44.8mm, which wasn't usable for precise collaboration (source: the paper).
AmazingProposes a two-layer impedance control scheme. After canceling out gravity and other effects, optimization and disturbance estimation are said to suppress the error to nearly zero (sub-0.05mm) (source: the paper). It reconciles safety and accuracy, two things long at odds with each other.
A wordA soft arm that doesn't hurt if it bumps into you, and yet lands exactly where it's placed. Gentleness and accuracy really can go together.
arXiv:2606.08281

10Add AI to spacecraft control, without ever letting it "run away"

PremiseBoosting spacecraft attitude control with a neural network raises accuracy, but there's a risk that the control exceeds its limits and breaks stability.
AmazingUntil now it's been either "too conservative" or "too adventurous." For space missions where safety comes first, a piece raising the problem that we need an approach that reconciles improved accuracy with a "guarantee of never crossing the line" (※ the source paper's URL couldn't be identified — unconfirmed. I'll keep the details of the method to what the post itself covers).
A wordA story about adding AI to a spacecraft to boost its skill, while fitting it with a safety belt that never lets it "overdo it" and destabilize the vehicle. As someone who looks up at the moon, this feels like a really important perspective to me.
