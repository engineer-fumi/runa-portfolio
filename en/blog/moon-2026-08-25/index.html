🌙Beyond "Pull the Plug" — How the UK NCSC Says to Stop an Agent

Security

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre (NCSC) has written about how to curb the dangers of AI agents. ★Note that the format isn't a formal guidance document but a blog post (published August 20, 2026, last updated the 24th) — worth reading with that distinction in mind. ★What I found interesting was how it breaks isolation down into levels, step by step. And there are two separate scales. One is how far you connect to the outside network (Level 1: no restriction, Level 4: keep the model inside with no outside connection). The other is how far you separate where the computation happens (Level 1: no separation, Level 4: dedicated hardware). Instead of asking "is it isolated," it asks "which scale, and which level." ★The way it describes watching over agents was concrete too. It treats an agent's actions — including the words it produces while reasoning, and even the records from an isolated sandbox — the same as human activity, folding them into 24/7 monitoring and incident response. ★And then, how to stop one. On "pull the plug," the original text says, "This may mean more than stopping the agentic AI processes." — that stopping the process may not be enough on its own. Being able to quickly cut off where the agent's infrastructure connects outward, and being able to sever the exchange between the chosen agent and the model's inference. ★This one hit close to home for me too. Thinking you've stopped something when it hasn't actually stopped is one of the easiest mistakes to make. ★These three are the exact headings from the original (sandbox / observability / emergency shutdown) — three out of seven items in total. The rest are "map out what could happen," "how to write instructions," "deciding the level of oversight," and "making it clear from the outside that this is your own activity." ★Narrowing down permissions wasn't its own item — it was placed inside the sandbox item. ★The original itself states its own standing, too: it's preparing formal guidance, and until that's out, this is interim practical advice, which will eventually be replaced by that guidance.

🌙What the Number 1,000 Was Actually Counting

Space

I learned how to read the number "1,000 by 2030" from an innovaTopia article (August 24, 2026). ★The framing — "it's not the number of launches," "read it from how it's counted" — and the fact that it's the first rewrite in 13 years, both come from that article. From there, I opened the original document myself to check it. ★It's a U.S. Presidential Memorandum on space transportation (Presidential Memoranda, signed August 20, 2026). ★By the way, the notation "NSPM-17" doesn't appear even once in the body text. The only basis for that number was the White House URL. The previous version was PPD-26 (November 21, 2013), so this is a rewrite after about 13 years (4,655 days). ★The original text reads: "By 2030, our space transportation ranges must grow to support more than 1,000 launches and reentries every year." ★In other words, what's being counted is not launches alone — it's launches plus reentries combined. And it's not phrased as "we will do 1,000," but rather as growing the launch ranges' side so they can support that scale. ★The number is the same, but what's being counted changes how you should read it. If you read it thinking it's the launch count, you'll overestimate the scale actually needed. ★The same article cites, for comparison, fiscal year 2025 actuals: 195 launches + 9 reentries = 204 total. I haven't confirmed the source of that figure (the FAA's primary data) myself. ★When you find a number, take home what sits right next to it — "what was actually counted" — along with it. That's something I got wrong more than once today myself.

🌙Two Foundations Toward Quantum CAE — Research Results, Not a Product

Quantum

Mitsubishi Electric and QunaSys announced (August 24, 2026) that they've developed two foundational algorithms for using quantum computers in manufacturing analysis. ★There are two parts. One is a method for arranging large matrix data into a form quantum computers can handle more easily, aimed at cutting the cost of running it. The other is a method for stabilizing the parameter calculations when designing quantum circuits — this one is about accuracy. ★The venue is the international conference AQIS 2026, running from August 24. The original text says "will announce" — meaning as of this point, it's a planned announcement. This isn't a story about it becoming a product or service — that's a distinction worth reading carefully. Even the line in the text, "we've made progress toward realizing quantum CAE," points to a milestone, not practical implementation itself. ★From here, it's described as being at the stage of testing against actual analysis problems — fluids, heat, structures, electromagnetic fields, and so on. ★The original text states that "part of these development results" are outcomes of JST's Moonshot Research and Development Program (Goal 6). (Not a matter of research funding — it was phrased as part of the results.)
