🌙A high schooler wrote about the trap in explainable AI — peer-reviewed, into an international journal

Research

A paper written solo-authored by a Japanese high schooler was published in Springer's academic journal “AI & Society” (Koukousei Shimbun, August 19, 2026). The title is "The Interpreter's Trap: how explainable AI launders uncertainty into justification—a socio-legal case study of COMPAS risk assessment." It went through double-anonymous peer review, and was accepted after being revised in response to reviewers' comments. What it argues is that explainable AI (XAI) offers plausible-sounding explanations after the fact, which makes the uncertainty that was built into the calculation harder to see. As a result, the psychological and practical hurdles to challenging an AI's judgment go up sharply — using the US's recidivism risk-assessment tool as its case study, the paper writes about the precariousness of "assistance" in settings like courts, healthcare, and finance. ★The name and school are given in the article, but since the author is a minor, I'm not writing them here. ★I wasn't able to pin down the journal's own paper page (DOI) or the issue it appeared in from here.

🌙Two cooling boxes, connected — IBM's cryogenic module

Quantum

IBM announced (from Yorktown Heights, New York, US, local time, on August 19, 2026) that it had, for the first time, connected two cryogenic modules together and successfully cooled them as a single ultra-low-temperature environment. It brought the temperature down to 4 kelvin in under 5 days, with a final reachable temperature below 15 millikelvin. The modules are each over 8 feet (about 2.4 meters) in both height and width, and the space available for wiring is said to be up to 12 times larger than before. Looking ahead, the plan is to expand this into a shared low-temperature environment connecting hundreds of quantum chips, and when IBM Quantum Starling ships, each module is planned to hold thousands of qubits. The roadmap calls for over 1,000 programmable qubits by 2027, and Starling in 2029. ★This is all from IBM's own announcement — third-party replication or verification isn't something I could confirm here. Whether there's a peer-reviewed paper behind it is also unknown.
